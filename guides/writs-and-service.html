Writ petitions and service matters

Article 226 permits the High Court to enforce fundamental rights and other legal rights, subject to public-law maintainability. Article 32 before the Supreme Court concerns enforcement of fundamental rights; it is not a general remedy for every legal-right dispute.

Stage by stage

  1. Representation

    A written representation to the authority is usually made first, and its rejection or silence is placed on record.

    Constitution of India

  2. Alternative remedy

    Where an effective statutory remedy exists the court may ask you to use it first, though a writ may still lie in certain cases (for example breach of natural justice or lack of jurisdiction).

    Whirlpool Corporation v. Registrar of Trade Marks, (1998) 8 SCC 1, Supreme Court of India, decided 26.10.1998 · Constitution of India

  3. Forum for service matters

    Many service disputes of central government employees go first to the Central Administrative Tribunal (s.14, s.19 Administrative Tribunals Act); tribunal decisions are subject to the High Court.

    L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India, (1997) 3 SCC 261, Supreme Court of India, decided 18.03.1997 · Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985

  4. Territorial jurisdiction

    A High Court can hear the petition if the cause of action arose wholly or partly within its territory (Art. 226(2)).

    Kusum Ingots & Alloys Ltd. v. Union of India, (2004) 6 SCC 254, Supreme Court of India, decided 28.04.2004 · Constitution of India

  5. Notice, reply and hearing

    If the petition is entertained, notice issues; the State files a reply and the matter is heard.

    Constitution of India

What to prepare

  • Keep copies of the impugned order and all representations.
  • Note dates carefully; delay can defeat a writ.
  • Collect service records, appointment letters and rules relied upon.

Documents to keep ready

  • Impugned order
  • Representations and replies
  • Service book extracts / appointment letters
  • Relevant rules and policies

Frequently asked questions

Is there a time limit for a writ?

There is no fixed limitation period, but unexplained delay and laches can lead the court to decline relief.

Can excess salary paid to me be recovered?

The Supreme Court in State of Punjab v. Rafiq Masih listed situations in which recovery from employees would be impermissible.
