Arbitration

Arbitration is a private method of deciding disputes under an arbitration agreement, governed by the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996.

Arbitration

Last reviewed 11 October 2026

Published by Master Legal Work · Sources and use of this guide

Stage by stage

  1. Notice invoking arbitration

    A party invokes the arbitration clause by notice, proposing an arbitrator where required.

    Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996

  2. Interim measures

    Before or during arbitration, the court may grant interim measures (s.9); the tribunal also has powers (s.17).

    Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996

  3. Appointment

    If the parties do not agree on the arbitrator, the High Court or Supreme Court may appoint one (s.11).

    Vidya Drolia v. Durga Trading Corporation, AIRONLINE 2020 SC 929, Supreme Court of India, decided 14.12.2020 · Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996

  4. Proceedings and award

    Pleadings, evidence and arguments follow; the award is generally to be made within the time in s.29A.

    Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996

  5. Challenge

    An award may be challenged only on limited grounds (s.34), within three months from receipt of the signed award (or disposal of a Section 33 request, where applicable). On sufficient cause, the Court may allow a further thirty days, but not thereafter (s.34(3)).

    Ssangyong Engineering & Construction Co. v. NHAI, AIR 2019 SC 5041, Supreme Court of India, decided 08.05.2019 · Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996

  6. Enforcement

    After the Section 34 filing period expires, an award is enforceable like a decree under s.36, subject to any stay order. Filing a Section 34 challenge does not automatically stay enforcement: a separate stay application and order are required.

    Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996

What to prepare

  • Locate the signed contract and the arbitration clause.
  • Note the seat / venue and the appointment procedure in the clause.
  • Keep the correspondence that shows the dispute arose.

Documents to keep ready

  • Contract with arbitration clause
  • Invoices and correspondence
  • Notice invoking arbitration
  • Any interim orders

Frequently asked questions

Can every dispute go to arbitration?

No. Some disputes are not arbitrable; the Supreme Court explained the test in Vidya Drolia.

On what grounds can an award be set aside?

Only on the limited grounds in s.34, which the Supreme Court interpreted narrowly after the 2015 amendment in Ssangyong Engineering.
