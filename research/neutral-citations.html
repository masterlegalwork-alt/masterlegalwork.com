Neutral citations

A neutral citation identifies a judgment by court, year and number, independent of any law report. Here is how the Supreme Court and the Punjab & Haryana High Court use them.

On this page

  • Supreme Court
  • P&H High Court
  • How to search
  • Citing in pleadings

Supreme Court of India

FormatYYYY INSC NNN: year, the court code INSC, and a running number for the year.
Verified examplesPankaj Bansal v. Union of India, 2023 INSC 866
Raj Reddy Kallem v. State of Haryana, 2024 INSC 347 (decided 08.04.2024)
Where it appearsAt the top of the first page of the judgment as published by the Supreme Court.

Punjab & Haryana High Court

FormatYYYY:PHHC:NNNNNN: year, the court code PHHC, and a six-digit running number. Division Bench judgments carry the suffix -DB. Used from 2023.
Verified examplesRohit Kumar v. State of Punjab, CRM-M-13900-2021, decided 03.04.2024: 2024:PHHC:044297
Division Bench, MRC-1-2020, decided 04.03.2024: 2024:PHHC:029710-DB
Where it appearsIn the header of the judgment or order as uploaded by the High Court.

How to search

  1. Supreme Court: open eSCR (scr.sci.gov.in) and search by neutral citation, party name or text; or browse judgments by date and by case number.
  2. P&H High Court: use the judgment search on the High Court website, which accepts the neutral citation, or the case type, number and year.
  3. Across High Courts: eCourts judgment search lets you search by text and filter by court and date.

Citing in pleadings

Give the neutral citation alongside the law-report citation where both exist, for example: Pankaj Bansal v. Union of India, 2023 INSC 866. Always cite from the court’s own copy and check whether the judgment has been followed, distinguished or overruled.

Verified 9 October 2026. Each example above was checked against the judgment header. General information only.
